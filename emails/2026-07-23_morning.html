– Stocks flat as Iran tensions rise; Brent crude nears $95 on infrastructure fears (wsj) Major indexes were mixed as geopolitical risk overshadowed earnings: the S&P 500 fell 0.1%, the Nasdaq Composite slid 0.6%, and the Dow shed just 6 points. Brent crude touched $95 a barrel, now roughly $20 higher than earlier this month, after President Trump threatened to strike Iran's infrastructure if Tehran attacks shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. The WSJ notes rising threats to energy infrastructure globally, from Hormuz to the Black Sea, with oil central to two major conflicts. In single-stock news, GE Vernova tumbled more than 8%, leading S&P decliners, despite raising revenue and margin guidance on a surge in new orders — a sign investor expectations had run too hot. Conversely, Super Micro Computer surged nearly 20%, topping S&P gainers, after forecasting quarterly gross margins twice as high as previously guided; peer Dell Technologies jumped more than 9% on the read-through for server demand. The divergence highlights how AI-infrastructure spending continues to reward server makers while punishing even strong results elsewhere. Investors should watch for further Iran-related escalation and its impact on oil and shipping lanes, along with additional earnings from energy and tech names for signs of whether the AI capex cycle and geopolitical risk premium persist.

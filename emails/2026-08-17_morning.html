– Retail sales fall 0.6% in July, consumer sentiment drops 8%, signaling US spending slowdown. (wsj) Fresh cracks in the American consumer emerged Friday as U.S. retail sales fell 0.6% in July, badly missing the expected 0.1% gain, though analysts noted the shift in Amazon's Prime Day timing may have distorted the figure. Compounding the concern, the preliminary August University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment Index dropped 8%, erasing two months of improvement. Combined with July's weak jobs report, the data suggest the labor market and household spending may be entering a soft patch, raising the stakes for upcoming Fed policy decisions. Meanwhile, oil prices jumped after a tanker was attacked leaving the Persian Gulf and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent threatened tighter sanctions on Iran's economy; Brent crude rose 1.67% to $88.52/barrel, up nearly 6% for the week. Treasury yields extended their climb amid mounting fiscal worries, with the 10-year yield up 0.056 point to 4.695%, following Thursday's weak 30-year bond auction that priced at a 5.216% yield, the highest in 25 years. Rising long-term borrowing costs are pressuring rate-sensitive equity sectors and reviving debate over U.S. debt sustainability. Investors should watch next week's Fed commentary, additional consumer data, and Middle East tensions for further direction on rates and oil.

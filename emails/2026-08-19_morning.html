– 30-year Treasury yield hits 5.337%, highest since 2007; Nasdaq falls 1.3% on chip selloff (wsj) Markets sold off broadly as the 30-year Treasury yield spiked to 5.337%, its highest intraday level since 2007, driven by fiscal deficit concerns, inflation worries, and the AI industry's massive debt appetite competing with sovereign borrowers for capital. Japanese long-dated yields also hit multi-decade highs. The Nasdaq dropped 1.3%, the S&P 500 fell 0.7%, and the Dow slipped 0.2%. Chip stocks were hit hardest, with the PHLX Semiconductor index plunging 5% and several components down 7% or more, reflecting rate-sensitivity and valuation concerns in the AI-linked trade. Klarna shares crashed 23%, their worst day since February, after the buy-now-pay-later firm cut 2026 revenue guidance below Wall Street estimates, citing softer consumer discretionary spending in Germany (its largest market) and currency translation drag, overshadowing otherwise strong Q2 results. Adding to risk-off sentiment, an unidentified projectile struck a cargo ship in the Strait of Hormuz overnight after the 60-day U.S.-Iran truce expired with no permanent deal in sight, pushing Brent crude to around $91/barrel. Investors will watch for further Treasury auction demand signals, geopolitical developments in the Gulf, and whether chip-sector weakness spreads to broader tech leadership.

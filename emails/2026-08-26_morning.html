– S&P 500 up 0.3% as oil drops 3.9%; Dick's Sporting Goods craters 31% on weak sneaker sales (wsj) Major indexes closed mostly higher, with the S&P 500 up 0.3%, the Nasdaq gaining 0.6%, and the Dow adding 160 points (0.3%). Brent crude fell 3.9% to $88.58/barrel as traders welcomed signs the Trump administration favors economic rather than military pressure on Iran, easing inflation fears and pulling the 10-year Treasury yield down 0.065 point to 4.638%. In trade news, Canada announced retaliatory tariffs of 15%-50% on roughly $20 billion of U.S. goods, about 7% of total U.S. imports, effective Sept. 8, doubling existing steel and aluminum tariffs to 50% and targeting items including motorcycles, cheese, and appliances. The biggest single-stock story was Dick's Sporting Goods, which plunged 31% after cutting its outlook, blaming weak sneaker demand and heavy promotional activity tied to its Foot Locker business as rivals slash prices to move inventory. The selloff dragged down sportswear and retail peers including Nike, Lululemon, Under Armour, and Target, all falling on read-through concerns about consumer footwear spending. Investors will watch for further details on Canada's tariff implementation, any U.S. countermeasures, and whether other retailers confirm the industry-wide demand weakness flagged by Dick's.

– Stocks flat as Treasury yields hit multi-decade highs, oil surges to $106 on Mideast tensions (wsj) Major indexes opened lower but rallied midday on reports of possible U.S.-Iran talks over a phased deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, plus an ongoing Trump-Xi summit touted as making 'tremendous strides.' By the close, moves were muted: the S&P 500 slipped slightly, the Nasdaq Composite edged up, and the Dow fell 161 points (-0.3%). Treasurys sold off further, with 10-year yields closing at 5.16% and 30-year yields at 5.46%, both the highest since the 2000s, while 2-year yields neared 4.9%, a level last seen in May 2024; a smaller-than-expected Treasury buyback of long-term bonds Thursday added to the pressure. The MOVE index, a gauge of bond volatility, spiked to levels unseen since early in the Iran war, raising fears of wider corporate bond spreads ahead. Brent crude jumped another $3.52 to $106/barrel after Saudi Arabia intercepted Houthi missiles, stoking concern that Red Sea shipping routes could also be disrupted. MGM Resorts shares plunged 11% after Barry Diller withdrew his bid to buy the remaining stake in the casino operator. Investors will watch for concrete outcomes from the Trump-Xi summit and any confirmation of U.S.-Iran progress, alongside further moves in yields and oil that could pressure equity valuations.

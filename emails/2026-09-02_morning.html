* US MARKET OVERNIGHT
– Oil tops $94/bbl on Mideast strikes; yields surge to multiyear highs; S&P falls 0.7% (wsj) Brent crude surged above $94/barrel Tuesday, extending Monday's move past $90, after the U.S. said it struck Iranian targets in response to attacks on commercial ships and U.S. personnel in the region. The renewed Middle East conflict, combined with fears of oil-driven inflation, sent bond yields sharply higher worldwide: Japan's 10-year yield hit levels unseen since 1996, German and U.K. 10-year yields reached multiyear highs, and the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield climbed to its highest since January 2025. Equities sold off broadly on the combination of geopolitical risk and higher-for-longer rate expectations: the S&P 500 fell 0.7%, the Nasdaq dropped 1%, and the Dow lost 419 points (-0.8%). Energy-sensitive and rate-sensitive sectors were hit hardest as oil's spike raised stagflation concerns just as central banks continue fighting inflation. Adding to the murky macro picture, Labor Department data showed job openings rose slightly in July versus June, but actual hiring softened, suggesting employers are growing more cautious even as demand for workers holds up on paper. Investors will watch for further escalation in the Middle East, oil's trajectory toward/past $95-100, and upcoming Fed commentary for clues on how policymakers weigh energy-driven inflation risk against slowing labor demand.
